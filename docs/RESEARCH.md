# Research and original contribution

## Problem grounding

The World Bank's 28 October 2022 Pakistan flood assessment reports 33 million affected people. This motivates studying transparent relief decisions. It is not a live data source or evidence of this prototype's effectiveness.

https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2022/10/28/pakistan-flood-damages-and-economic-losses-over-usd-30-billion-and-reconstruction-needs-over-usd-16-billion-new-assessme

## Established research

Models for relief routing: Equity, efficiency and efficacy examines the tension between transportation efficiency, response efficacy and distribution equity.
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1366554511000639

Equitable last mile distribution in emergency response discusses fairness measures in relief distribution.
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360835218305655

Public abstracts were consulted. No claim to have reviewed inaccessible full texts is made. No data or verbatim passages from these papers are incorporated into the demo.

## Alternatives considered

| Direction | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Generic scholarship or study chatbot | Easy demo, but weak differentiation and source reliability dependent on external services. |
| Real-time flood route predictor | Attractive map, but unsupported safety claims and unavailable validated road data. |
| Relief allocation exercise | Real problem, deterministic mathematical behavior, measurable trade-offs, no private data or paid AI dependency. |

Selected RelayPlan because its functional evidence is directly visible: exact integer counts, route constraints, baseline differences and closure sensitivity.

## Originality

The fairness algorithm is not new. The original project combines a private scenario workflow, an inspectable three-level objective, clearly labeled greedy comparator, non-mutating disruption previews, exportable audits and a simple independent oracle. No SATOpen or Daanish Alumni code was used.

## Impact plan

Not yet field-tested. Next: interview relief volunteers about their planning workflow, run tabletop tasks with sample scenarios, measure task completion and allocation comprehension, and revise assumptions with domain experts.

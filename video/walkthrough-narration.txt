RelayPlan is a relief planning simulator. This is the actual app. The default exercise has two depots, one hundred and eighty kits, and three hundred kits of demand. All places and numbers are fictional. Start with the allocation planner. Every community receives sixty percent of its demand. Here are the exact counts and the unmet need. Now compare strategies. The nearest-first heuristic delivers the same number of kits, but Orchard receives none. RelayPlan protects the lowest coverage, with more transport effort. That trade-off stays visible. Let's change North depot's stock. The plan recalculates immediately. Restore the sample, then open a route. Capacity and travel estimates are editable, and routes can be blocked. In Stress Test, preview closing South depot to Orchard. The lowest coverage falls from sixty to twenty-five percent. The preview leaves the original plan intact. Apply the closure to see the blocked route and revised allocations. Switch to a shortage exercise, then reset. Import a scenario to reproduce your own inputs. Finally, How it works explains the three objectives, the research and the model's limits. We checked the solver against independent exhaustive enumeration. This is a learning and tabletop prototype, not a live dispatch system. RelayPlan makes each shortfall visible, so a person can review the decision.
